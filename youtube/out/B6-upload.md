**Title:** Why crowds feel stressful: your brain's personal space

**Description:**
```
Why does being packed into a crowd feel so stressful? One idea is personal space. Your brain keeps an invisible bubble around you and notices when someone gets too close. In a brain imaging study, healthy volunteers showed more activity in the amygdala, the brain's alarm system, when another person was brought closer. And a woman with damage to both amygdalae had almost no sense of personal space and was comfortable standing far closer than most people. A crowd breaks that bubble again and again, so the alarm may keep firing. These studies did not test crowds directly, so treat this as an explanation, not a diagnosis.

Source: Kennedy, Gläscher, Tyszka & Adolphs, 2009, Nature Neuroscience, "Personal space regulation by the human amygdala".
```
